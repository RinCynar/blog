> **Introduction**: In the everyday context of consumer electronics and audiophile circles, "High Fidelity" (Hi-Fi) is often vaguely defined as "faithfully reproducing sound." However, when we strip away commercial rhetoric through the lenses of acoustic engineering, information theory, and human physiology to push this concept to its physical boundaries, an unsettling dilemma immediately surfaces: **Which exact "fidelity" is High Fidelity actually preserving?**
> If sound masking caused by physical laws is considered an intrinsic part of sound, should the unmitigated air conditioning noise in a recording studio also be faithfully recorded and reproduced? If what we seek is "live sound as heard by the creator," yet creators never publicly disclose their workstation setups, power sources, or monitoring environments, is an audiophile's obsession with gold-plated cables, wall outlets, or even "hydro vs. wind power" grounded in rationality or theology? Furthermore, if absolute fidelity is a mirage from the very beginning at the level of human physiological structure, how should we understand the phenomenon where technological "imperfections"—outdated by progress—are sanctified as the "soul" within cultural communities?
> Starting from the tripartite ontological division of Hi-Fi, this article explores the engineering boundary between signal compensation and distortion, ultimately extending to the semiotic evolution between Vocaloid and SynthV—revealing how the human brain deploys "aesthetic rhetoric" during technological iterations to elevate habit and defect into artistic commitment.

## I. Ontological Schism: Which "Fidelity" Is High Fidelity Actually Preserving?

At the intersection of acoustic engineering and artistic philosophy, "sound" is not a single-layer physical phenomenon, but a complex mapping chain. As a result, the concept of "truth/fidelity" in Hi-Fi logically splits into three mutually contradictory dimensions:

```
                          ┌─── 1. Physical / Signal Fidelity
Tripartite Division ──────┼─── 2. Intentional / Artistic Fidelity
  of High Fidelity        └─── 3. Perceptual / Subjective Fidelity

```

### 1. Physical Fidelity and the "Boundary Ethics of Noise"

If we define high fidelity as "the indiscriminate and faithful reproduction of physical vibrations in the air," we immediately fall into the first logical paradox: while instruments and vocal cords vibrate according to physical laws to produce sound, the ambient noise of studio air conditioners, power frequency hums from electronics, and the sticky sounds of a singer's saliva are equally products of physical laws. Treating all physical existences as targets of fidelity clearly violates the audience's definition of "music." But if we selectively filter out interferences, where should engineering and playback equipment draw the line?

#### (1) Ethics of Playback Equipment: No Right to Exercise "Aesthetic Supervision"

In the ethics of signal reproduction systems, **a Hi-Fi device is a "channel," not an "artistic judge."**

* **The Master File is the Absolute Boundary**: During the mixing and post-production stages, mix engineers use plugins to eliminate noise. Even if 10% of the air conditioner noise remains due to algorithmic limitations, the moment this audio file is exported as the final Master tape, that remaining 10% officially becomes the "physical inventory" of that digital signal.
* **The Duty of Reproduction**: The sole duty of playback equipment is to faithfully reproduce every single sample point in the file. If a playback system presumptuously "erases" that 10% residual noise, it is effectively usurping the role of the mix engineer. If it erases air conditioner noise today, it might misinterpret and erase the subtle bow-hair friction of a cello as noise tomorrow.

#### (2) The Ontological Difference Between LDAC and final 8K Sound

This boundary directly clarifies the confusion present in consumer audio technology:

* **LDAC (Transport Layer Lossless)**: Belongs to the realm of **Physical / Signal Fidelity**. Its essence is widening the bitrate ceiling of wireless transmission channels (up to 990 kbps) to reduce data loss at the physical transport layer and maintain digital signal integrity.
* **final 8K Sound (Perceptual Layer Reconstruction)**: Belongs to the realm of **Psychoacoustics and Phase Rendering**. It does not preserve losslessness at the transport layer; rather, it uses algorithms to reconstruct high-frequency phase and micro-dynamics while sharpening edges, thereby creating a perceptual illusion of "rich detail" at the psychoacoustic level.
* **Boundary Summary**: The former is "fidelity" (transmitting the signal intact), while the latter is an "audio effect / rendering" (reconstructing the auditory experience at the perceptual end via algorithms). Conflating the two is the result of audio marketing long blurring engineering boundaries.

### 2. Intentional Fidelity and Mystical Compensation

If high fidelity is defined as "reproducing the sound the creator heard in the studio," this proposition immediately collapses in practice into an unverifiable closed loop.

#### (1) The Unverifiable Live Scene

Music producers' working environments vary wildly: different studio monitors (e.g., the frequency response characteristics of the Yamaha NS-10M vs. Genelec monitors are vastly different), acoustically treated rooms, or even physiological high-frequency attenuation in mix engineers caused by prolonged exposure to high sound pressure levels. Almost no artist publicly releases their workstation hardware models, power supply systems, or room HRTF correction parameters alongside an album. To take a more extreme assumption, some creators even use non-professional gear or apply personal tuning EQs during production.

#### (2) The Psychological Mechanism of Technological Superstition

Faced with the reality that subjective intent cannot be reproduced, the Hi-Fi community has birthed fervent debates regarding "gold-plated 3.5mm jacks," "specially woven copper cables," "specific batches of low-speed Sony memory cards," and even the impact of "hydro, wind, or nuclear power" on sound quality.

From the perspective of Information Theory, digital audio consists entirely of discrete 0s and 1s before entering the Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC). As long as data validation is error-free and clock jitter remains within tolerance, neither the read speed of the storage medium nor the generation method of the upstream power supply can alter the binary structure of the waveform. The audiophile's mystical pursuit of uncontrollable physical variables is essentially a **technological compensation and psychological comfort mechanism** deployed when facing the impossibility of reproducing the creator's original intent.

### 3. Perceptual Fidelity and the Physiological Barrier

The ultimate paradox of high fidelity occurs in the one-centimeter space where sound crosses the headphone diaphragm and enters the human ear.

```
[Equipment Waveform] ──► [Pinna / Ear Canal / Ossicles (HRTF Filtering)] ──► [Eardrum & Neural Signals (Unique to Everyone)]

```

#### (1) The Physical Barrier of HRTF

Every individual's pinna shape, ear canal curvature, and skull density are unique. In acoustics, the scattering and reflective filtering of sound caused by these physical structures is called the **Head-Related Transfer Function (HRTF)**. This means that even if an absolutely flat, zero-distortion audio system produces identical waveforms, the measured frequency response curve and phase will be drastically different when those waveforms hit different people's eardrums.

#### (2) The Essence of Fidelity Is "Decoding," Not "Correcting"

However, this does not mean that the pursuit of Hi-Fi fidelity is a mirage. When we hear an acoustic violin in real life, the sound waves are similarly refracted by our own ear structures. Over millions of years of evolution, the human brain has "hardwired" its ear anatomy into a default decoder.

* **The True Mission of Hi-Fi**: The mission of Hi-Fi equipment is not to correct the listener's ears, but **to recreate in front of the eardrum the original physical acoustic field that the sound source should produce in space.**
* **Conclusion**: Physiological structure is not an "interference" to be removed, but a necessary medium for the human ear to perceive the physical world. Attempting to bypass physiological characteristics to pursue an absolutely unified auditory "truth" is physically unrealistic in itself.

## II. Compensation and Intervention: Is EQ "Correction" or "Distortion"?

Having clarified the physical and physiological boundaries of Hi-Fi, we must confront a concrete engineering practice question: **Does using an equalizer (EQ) or DSP tools for intervention in the playback chain make the system "more Hi-Fi" or "less Hi-Fi"?**

Here, a cold engineering boundary must be established: **The demarcation between De-distortion (inverse compensation) and Coloration (subjective enhancement).**

```
                         ┌─── Inverse Compensation (De-distortion) ──► Offsets physical flaws to flatten response ──► More Hi-Fi
Equalizer (EQ) Dual Role ┤
                         └─── Subjective Coloration (Audio Effects)  ──► Deviates from flat response to suit taste  ──► Less Hi-Fi

```

### 1. Inverse Compensation (De-distortion): A Hi-Fi Method to Eliminate Distortion

When physical size limitations cause miniature speakers in smartphones or tablets to suffer severe acoustic attenuation below 200 Hz, the audio output by the system already exhibits severe physical distortion.

* If software like FxSound applies an inverse filtering curve to the low frequencies to restore a flat frequency response curve that approaches the performance of studio monitor headphones;
* **Engineering Judgment**: This action neutralizes the distortion caused by the physical limitations of the hardware. In acoustic logic, it constitutes "inverse compensation," and its result **makes the overall reproduction system closer to Hi-Fi standards.**

### 2. Tuning Coloration (Audio Effects): Audio Artifice Outside of Fidelity

If the reproduction system is already capable of flatly reproducing the Master file's information, but the listener boosts the low frequencies by 6 dB or cuts high-frequency harshness purely to chase "bass impact" or "warm vocals":

* **Engineering Judgment**: This action disrupts the physical and signal-level proportions of the original Master, representing a secondary creation based on personal aesthetic preference. It enters the domain of **DSP Tuning / Audio Effects**, separating itself from the engineering definition of Hi-Fi (fidelity).

## III. Aesthetic Domestication: When "Technical Crude Quality" Is Elevated into "Soul"

This dialectical struggle over physical imperfections, engineering limitations, and the concept of "fidelity" exists not only in audiophile amplifiers and cables, but is also **strikingly displayed in modern digital culture—most notably across the evolutionary history of VOCALOID and SynthV (Synthesizer V).**

### 1. Cold Engineering Realities: Crude Splicing vs. Deep Neural Networks

From a pure speech synthesis engineering standard, the difference between VOCALOID and SynthV is not a distinction between two artistic schools, but rather **a physical technological gap caused by generational leaps in computing power and algorithms.**

| Dimension | VOCALOID (Represented by the V2/V3 Era) | SynthV (Synthesizer V) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Underlying Algorithm** | Phoneme Concatenation Synthesis (Unit Selection / PSOLA) | Deep Neural Network Probability Models (DNN-based AI) |
| **Vocal Characteristics** | Frequency band disjoints, metallic resonance noise, clumsy mechanical legato | Realistic breathiness, pharyngeal tones, vocal cord friction, and natural micro-vibrato |
| **Engineering Position** | **An extremely crude mechanical imitation of human voice** | **A hyper-realistic simulacrum exceeding human vocal limits** |

In 2007, when Hatsune Miku exploded in popularity, YAMAHA's official team had no masterstroke design intended "to deliberately retain robotic artifacts to chase an inhuman feel," nor did they hold a preconceived stance to forge a cybernetic symbol. The engineering team's ultimate goal was crystal clear: **smooth out the discontinuities as much as possible to approach human singing voice.** The so-called metallic cutting sounds and mechanical robotic tones were purely engineering compromises caused by YAMAHA's limited technology, insufficient computing power, and crude concatenation algorithms at the time.

### 2. Cognitive Dissonance and the Activation of Aesthetic Rhetoric

However, when technology advanced to the SynthV era and Dreamtonics finally fixed the crude flaws of the early engines—enabling voices to sing in ways that exceeded human lung capacity and vocal limits—a significant portion of the VOCALOID community experienced intense aversion.

This phenomenon can be deconstructed using a complete socio-psychological chain:

```
[Engineering Flaws / Robotic Artifacts] ──► [Auditory Binding in Youth] ──► [Arrival of New Tech (SynthV)] ──► [Cognitive Dissonance] ──► [Activation of Aesthetic Rhetoric / Sanctification]

```

1. **Aesthetic Domestication of Auditory Habits**: When a generation grows up forced to listen to voices with metallic artifacts, phoneme discontinuities, and mechanical clumsiness, their neural pathways are "domesticated" through prolonged, repeated stimulation. These auditory defects become forcibly bound to their youth memories and specific cultural experiences, transforming into **emotional anchors**.
2. **Cognitive Dissonance**: When complete, flawless AI voice technology arrives, the domesticated audience's habitual auditory experience faces denial. The domesticated group cannot logically accept the cold reality that "the soulful voice they loved for years was actually just a low-quality technical defect."
3. **Activation of Aesthetic Rhetoric**: To rationalize this dissonance, the brain deploys "aesthetic rhetoric"—**forcibly packaging "technical crude quality" as an "uniquely non-human soul," and elevating "habitual auditory dependency" into a "commitment to the aesthetics of the artificial."**

### 3. Recurring Trajectories in Cultural History: Forest Keepers vs. the New Era

This behavior of "aestheticizing technological defects" is a recurring motif in human cultural and technological evolution:

* **Vinyl and Film**: The "crackles" of vinyl records (physical noise from mechanical stylus friction against record dust) and the grain/light leaks of film photography were originally distortions caused by physical media defects. Yet in the digital lossless era, they have been sanctified by certain groups as "analog soul."
* **Pixel Art**: The pixel blocks in early 8-bit / 16-bit games were essentially engineering compromises under extreme VRAM scarcity on Famicom and Arcade hardware. When 4K rendering became mundane, pixel blocks were reverse-packaged into an avant-garde artistic style.
* **Vocaloid Robotic Artifacts**: In exact parallel to the two examples above, the robotic artifacts of V2 transformed in the eyes of veteran fans from "an unavoidable technical limitation" into a "specific cultural symbol."

**However, aesthetic rhetoric cannot halt the productivity laws of technology.** Nostalgic filters can retain the forest keepers of the old era, but they cannot attract a new generation of creators and listeners. As time progresses, new creators and producers unburdened by history will embrace modern AI engines like SynthV without hesitation due to their superior usability, expressiveness, and efficiency. The "robotic artifacts" of older engines will ultimately retreat into a niche symbol for a minority, just as vinyl records retreated into collector's items.

## Conclusion: Cold Realities and Romance Behind the Mirage

What, ultimately, is High Fidelity (Hi-Fi)?

Through the cold lens of engineering and physics, High Fidelity is an **asymptotic game pursuing signal integrity**. Absolute fidelity collapses into a mirage the moment it crosses physical equipment, room acoustics, and human physiological HRTF. The sole mission of playback equipment is to faithfully present the binary inventory within the Master file, with no right to subtract from art on its own accord.

Yet through the romantic lens of aesthetics and culture, human understanding of "sound" is filled with romantic self-deception. On one hand, we proclaim a pursuit of absolute physical reproduction; on the other hand, as technology smoothes away imperfections, we stubbornly cling to defects, packaging physical distortion as the imprint of the soul.

Perhaps this is precisely the most captivating aspect of audio and sound arts: **technology coldly dismantles every distortion and artificiality, while the human brain continuously uses nostalgia and aesthetic rhetoric to reinfuse soul into those broken flaws.**
