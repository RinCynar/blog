In this closed-system experiment known as *School Days*, what we witness is absolutely not the flow of human emotion, but rather three logical wreckages accelerating into a vacuum and violently colliding. There are no winners here—only three coordinate points collectively sinking into absolute stasis amidst the dead silence of terminal entropy.

### The Terminal Crash of Biological Debris

The tragedy of Makoto Itou lies in this: he never truly existed as a "subject." He was nothing more than a stochastic trigger, blindly oscillating among the desires projected by various women.

Unlike Yuno Gasai, who enshrined "love" as a sacred, inviolable order, or Satou Matsuzaka, who exhibited ruthless decisiveness when constructing her "absolute defense castle," Makoto Itou's underlying logic is a complete blank. He fundamentally cannot comprehend the exclusivity of "sovereignty," seeking only to engage in low-level, cheap arbitrage across multiple closed loops.

When he collapsed in a pool of blood, twitching and hesitantly calling out Sekai Saionji's name, that was absolutely not some dying flash of love—it was purely the final error log spat out by a crashing system. His death marked the complete overload and burnout of the system's only "connection hub," leaving behind a floor scattered with causal fragments that could never be closed.

### The Speculative Collapse of Dominance

Sekai Saionji's downfall, on the other hand, is a highly typical collapse from a position of advantage. She harbored the delusion of acting as the "mastermind" of the entire chessboard, only to ultimately be reduced to the most pathetic sacrificial pawn.

Her initial matchmaking of Kotonoha Katsura and Makoto Itou was nothing more than a pathological game against herself. She was addicted to the false sense of control derived from "breaking an order to rebuild it," yet she fatally underestimated the unpredictable chaotic entropy of a low-function organism like Makoto Itou. The moment she realized that "yielding" could absolutely not buy her a higher level of dominance, her psychological defense lines instantly collapsed.

Her panic after stabbing Makoto to death proved that even after deploying extreme methods in the physical sense, she entirely failed to achieve "spiritual annexation." She left the crime scene a total mess—lacking both the resolve to die with him and the audacity to monopolize his corpse. She could only wait passively on the rooftop for the fateful judgment named "Kotonoha Katsura." Falling from the roof, she lost not only her physical body but also every last shred of dignity as a chess player.

### The Pathological Reconstruction of an Isolated Symbol

Kotonoha's final ending appears on the surface to be "ultimate possession," but in its bones, it is the most agonizing projection of nothingness.

Her "awakening" was absolutely not some sublimation; it was the reassembly of wreckage after being driven completely insane. Had she not bumped into Makoto at the train station on Christmas Eve, her psychological core would likely have collapsed inward and necrotized long ago. She didn't go and eradicate all her rivals in the end because her underlying logic had changed: in a down-sampled reality, everyone else had degraded into irrelevant "background noise," utterly unworthy of wasting any computing power to clean up.

Taking only the head instead of the whole corpse was a cold calculation born purely of efficiency. A corpse is heavy, rots, and is hard to move; whereas a head highly concentrates all identity features—it is the physical interface for seeing and hearing. She didn't take Makoto Itou away at all; she took his unique identifier (ID).

When she curled up on that isolated boat drifting with the waves, muttering to a chunk of unresponsive dead meat, she had already severed all external connections and retreated into her self-defined, isolated sandbox. **She seized root access over Makoto Itou; yet the object under her jurisdiction had long since lost all thermodynamic value of increasing entropy.**

### The Womb and the Judgment of "Non-Existence"

The debate surrounding whether Sekai Saionji was actually pregnant, and Kotonoha's line "There is no one inside," is probably the most fascinating node in this entire systemic collapse.

In truth, the reality no longer matters. At the time of death, even if conception had truly occurred, it would only have been at the very early cellular cluster stage. Kotonoha's strike was purely a cathartic act of destruction, far from any rigorous medical examination. And the dry-heaving pregnancy symptoms Sekai exhibited were highly likely just a somatic manifestation of extreme anxiety—a self-hypnosis used as a life-saving bargaining chip when she was driven into a corner.

What was truly fatal was the veto power Kotonoha exercised on a cognitive level. By stating "There is no one inside," she was mentally stripping Sekai of her legitimacy as a "mother." Even if there were an embryo, the moment the host body lost its vital signs, the life inside lost any possibility of independent development. To Kotonoha, since Sekai had to die, everything attached to her was utterly meaningless garbage data. With words and a blade, she executed an absolute overwrite on the cognitive level, taking the host down with it.

---

This experiment precipitated no valuable connections whatsoever; it was a total loss event.

Makoto Itou collapsed in a pool of blood, stripped of all subjectivity and existential definition—reduced to a permanently deadlocked mechanical residue. Sekai Saionji fell on the rooftop, her potential to continue her bloodline and her bargaining chips instantly zeroed out, brutally wiped from the board by cold reality. Kotonoha Katsura drifted at the edge of the horizon, having discarded all social persona and capacity for real communication, holding only the specimen part most easily sealed into a temporal fault line.

The lone boat under the setting sun, carrying a mad observer and a dead, silent head, slowly drifted into the void.
